# First founder recording session

Status: proposed teaching outline for Orel's own delivery. No footage has been recorded or generated.

## Aim

Film two useful modules in one approximately 45-minute session. Preserve your natural teacher's voice. Work from short prompts rather than memorizing a polished marketing script. Leave a pause between chapters so they can be edited cleanly.

## Module 1 — How to learn at this Academy

Target finished length: 8–12 minutes.

| Chapter | Prompt for your own words | Primary placement |
|---|---|---|
| A personal welcome | Why did you want this Academy to exist? Who do you hope walks through its doors? | First visit and orientation |
| Start with the whole human | Why begin with the body, everyday life and careful observation before collecting remedies or techniques? | Foundation opening |
| Six schools, one education | What is each tradition contributing? How should a student connect them without pretending they are identical? | Academic plan and school introduction |
| Learn by doing | Describe one example of reading, observing, trying a safe educational exercise, reflecting and being able to explain it clearly | First practical lesson |
| Use the Scholar well | It helps explain material, locate sources and practice thinking. It does not replace a teacher's responsibility or establish facts by sounding confident | Scholar introduction |
| What earned progress means | Completing work, receiving feedback and demonstrating understanding; certificates recognize the stated Academy requirements | Orientation close |

Opening cue, not a script: "Before you learn a list of herbs, remedies or techniques, I want you to understand what you're looking at. That's where we begin here."

Avoid promises that programs, external accreditation, faculty access, live seminars or professional privileges are available until they actually are.

## Module 2 — The whole human: our first lesson

Target finished length: 10–15 minutes. Use a whiteboard or paper beside you.

| Chapter | Teaching task | Learning companion |
|---|---|---|
| Set the question | Explain what you want a beginner to understand about the body as connected systems | Clear learning objectives |
| Build one diagram | Draw a simple, accurate relationship between body systems; keep it readable and explain each connection | Clean verified illustration |
| Bring it into everyday life | Use an ordinary example such as movement, meals, sleep or attention to show why context matters | Reading card and observation prompts |
| Explain your thinking | Separate what is directly observed, what is inferred and what still needs a source | Source/evidence notes |
| Give one observation exercise | Ask students to observe and describe an everyday experience without diagnosing themselves or changing treatment | Practice worksheet |
| Teach it back | Ask the learner to explain the central idea in plain language and identify one question to investigate | Reflection and assessment |

Select the precise body-system content from the approved foundation material before filming. Do not improvise claims about disease mechanisms, treatment effects or historical quotations to fill the lesson.

## Simple recording setup

- Landscape frame; phone or existing camera at eye level; stable support.
- Quiet room, light facing you, uncluttered warm background.
- Make a short sound test first. Use existing equipment; no purchases required.
- Keep the same framing for both modules. Record diagrams separately if they are hard to read on camera.
- Pause, repeat a sentence if needed, and continue. You do not need a flawless take.
- Keep the full original recording. Do not send sensitive student/client stories or identifying details.

## Assets to produce from the recordings

1. Two complete teaching videos.
2. Focused chapter clips for genuinely relevant placements; no arbitrary clip quota.
3. Corrected captions and transcript, reviewed for meaning and factual accuracy.
4. Chapter titles and timestamps.
5. Still images of you teaching and a clean version of the whiteboard.
6. A transcript-to-lesson map for Scholar retrieval after editorial approval.

Suggested filenames: `ANW_01_Learning_at_the_Academy_original.mp4` and `ANW_02_Whole_Human_Foundation_original.mp4`.

## Current integration

The upgrade branch adds a Faculty Studio for attaching a supported HTTPS film URL to any existing lesson. It uses the existing `Lesson.videoUrl` field, so this change requires no new database schema. The lesson renders a click-to-load teaching player beside its written content. YouTube, Vimeo and direct MP4/WebM URLs are supported; no hosting account or paid plan has been created.

Caption and transcript production, hosting privacy, actual footage upload and transcript grounding remain to be completed after the recordings are supplied. Watching a video alone does not award WU or bypass the existing assessment.
